# Tuning plan 2026-09-30 — `TUNE-S2`, round four

**The evidence is one profiling record.** `docs/profiling/runs/` holds exactly one file,
`2026-09-28-meetings-detail-participants.md`, and every number in this plan derives from it. One
record is not a trend (`pipeline-tuning/SKILL.md:49-50`), so **every projected effect below inherits
that limit** and is marked projected. Nothing here is a comparison across runs, because there is
nothing to compare against.

**What this plan rests on:** `docs/profiling/reviews/2026-09-30-review.md` (`TUNE-S1`, accepted), 741
lines, itself resting on that one record plus the artifacts on disk. Section references below (§1–§12)
are that review's sections.

**What it explicitly does not rest on:** any measurement of the three previous tuning rounds.
`CH-020`, `CH-021` and `CH-022` all shipped on 2026-09-29 and **none has been measured** (§1). Ten of
the twenty-one inventory rows carry a figure; eleven do not (§2.6). `FEAT-S9`, `FEAT-S10`, `FEAT-S11`,
every `FIX-S*` row and the whole `TUNE` flow have zero data points.

**Why this round differs from the three before it.** §12 lists four levers that rounds one to three
refused on their own authority and never put to `TUNE-G1`: removing a review gate, dropping `FEAT-S6`
for small changes, changing a role's model, and defining a below-threshold `FEAT` path. The owner has
asked for exactly that class. All four appear below as numbered proposals with their savings and with
what they stop catching. Where I think a lever is a bad trade I say so **inside** the proposal as a
recommendation; the decision is `TUNE-G1`'s, not mine.

**The bias, applied in reverse.** The cheapest proposal to write is "add a step", and `CH-004` — 100
minutes of planning against 85 of code — is what that produced. Of the nine items below, six remove or
narrow something, two add something small to a record rather than to the flow, and one is a block of
corrections. Every addition carries its argument for why it is worth its cost on **every** future
change.

## P1 — A gate verdict classifies each blocker, and only a shape blocker re-runs the stage

### What changes

1. `.claude/skills/feature-pipeline/SKILL.md` — the four gate sections (§1 research review, §3 design
   review, §5 plan review, §7 code review) state that each blocker in a verdict is labelled either
   **`shape`** (the artifact's decisions change) or **`correction`** (the artifact is right and a
   statement in it is wrong, mispointed or stale).
2. `.claude/skills/team-roles/SKILL.md`, the hand-off/verdict format section — the verdict carries the
   label per blocker.
3. `.claude/skills/feature-pipeline/SKILL.md` §5 and `team-roles` — the response rule: a verdict
   containing **only** `correction` blockers is answered by a narrow dispatch to the owning role that
   names the blockers and the file and does **not** re-read the change folder; a verdict containing any
   `shape` blocker re-runs the stage as today.
4. `.claude/agents/research-reviewer.md`, `design-reviewer.md`, `plan-reviewer.md`, `code-reviewer.md`
   — the output contract gains the label. **No tool list and no model changes**, so `AR-API-07` and
   `AR-API-08` are untouched.

No stage, gate, role or document is added. `docs/process.md` does not change.

### The evidence

- Every one of the four gates returned `accept after blockers`; not one passed clean (§2.3, §2.5).
- Eight blockers in total, of which the record's own account is "**Two of the eight blockers changed
  the shape of the work; six were corrections to documents**" (§2.5).
- The per-gate "Changed the shape?" column reads **no** for `FEAT-G3` and **no** for `FEAT-G4` (§7.1).
- The rework those two gates caused: `FEAT-G3` → 169,549 tokens, `FEAT-G4` → 407,507 (§3, §7.1).
- Each gated stage shows exactly **2 dispatches** in the record — a first pass and one re-run (§2.1) —
  and a re-dispatched role re-reads the change folder, which was **2,717 lines** in the measured cycle,
  because "the change folder is the context, and it is read, not summarized"
  (`team-roles/SKILL.md:113-114`, §6).

### Expected effect — projected

**Upper bound: 577,056 tokens, 16.6% of the run.** (Arithmetic over §3's cited cells, performed here:
169,549 + 407,507; percentage against the record's 3,478,421.) That is the rework spent re-running
stages for verdicts whose own column says the shape did not change.

The realistic saving is a fraction of that bound and **is not projectable from one record**, because
the record carries no split between a re-run's re-reading cost and its productive edit (§3: "The
per-stage table does not split a row between first pass and re-run"). The bound is the honest ceiling,
not the estimate.

### What it costs, and what stops being caught

- **Nothing stops being caught.** The gates run unchanged, read the same artifacts and raise the same
  blockers. Only the response to a blocker changes.
- The addition is a label per blocker — a few words in a verdict that is already written. That is the
  full per-change cost, and it is paid on every gated change.
- **The real risk is misclassification.** A reviewer that labels a shape blocker `correction` gets a
  narrow fix where a re-think was needed. The mitigation available without adding anything: the label
  is the reviewer's, and `lead` holds the gate — a verdict whose `correction` blocker turns out to
  change a decision is escalated the way a disputed test already is
  (`feature-pipeline/SKILL.md:264-269`).
- **A second, subtler risk:** the record cannot distinguish whether the four `accept after blockers`
  verdicts mean the gates catch real defects or the stages run loosely because a gate is known to
  follow (§2.3, §11). If the second reading is true, a cheaper response to blockers makes the stages
  looser still. Nothing in one record settles this.

### How the next record shows whether it worked

`docs/profiling/README.md:28-32` already requires a re-run to get a **second row under the same stage
ID**. So the first record written after this change carries, for each gated stage, a first-pass row and
a re-run row. **The test: for a verdict labelled `correction`-only, the re-run row is at or below 25% of
its first-pass row's tokens.** In the measured cycle the equivalent ratios were `FEAT-S3` 169,549
against a 304,155 total row and `FEAT-S1` 183,652 against 303,500 — roughly half. If the next record's
correction-only re-run rows are still near half, P1 did not work and should be reverted.

### Do nothing

The gates keep returning blockers and each one keeps costing a full stage re-run. Rework stays at
whatever share the next record shows; 29.3% is the only figure we have.

## P2 — Remove one or more of the four review gates

**This is the lever §12 records as refused three times without ever reaching the owner.** It is
presented as four independent decisions. Approving any one does not commit the owner to the others.

### The numbers, per gate

Cost, blockers and yield from §7.1; rework from §3; sums and percentages are arithmetic over those
cells, performed here.

| Option | Gate      | Its own cost          | Rework it caused | Cost + rework     | Changed the shape? | What it found, verbatim from the record                                          |
| ------ | --------- | --------------------- | ---------------- | ----------------- | ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 2a     | `FEAT-G1` | 158,585 tok / 8.2 min | 183,652          | 342,237 (9.8%)    | yes — one          | "found two security cases the sweep never opened; they constrained the 404 body" |
| 2b     | `FEAT-G2` | 151,795 tok / 8.0 min | 257,359          | 409,154 (11.8%)   | yes — one          | "found the seed-mirror guarantee was vacuous; the fix made it real"              |
| 2c     | `FEAT-G3` | 126,135 tok / 6.1 min | 169,549          | 295,684 (8.5%)    | **no**             | "both were commit-ordering errors; cheap to fix, expensive to discover later"    |
| 2d     | `FEAT-G4` | 159,360 tok / 5.8 min | 407,507          | 566,867 (16.3%)   | **no**             | "both prose; the code was correct on every point pressed"                        |
| all    | four      | 595,875 / 28.1 min    | 1,018,067        | 1,613,942 (46.4%) | —                  | —                                                                                |

**A warning about the right-hand column.** Removing a gate saves its own cost with certainty —
595,875 tokens (17.1%) for all four, 28.1 of 190 agent-minutes. It does **not** save the rework. The
rework was spent fixing real defects; without the gate the defects are not fixed, they ship. The
"cost + rework" column is the cost of the gate **plus what fixing its findings cost**, not an
available saving. Read it as: this is how much of the run each gate governs.

### The rest of the evidence, cutting both ways

**For removing:** across all 36 `FX-` rows in the ledger, exactly **one** names a review gate in the
"Found by" column — `FX-004`, and jointly with acceptance (§7.2). No `FX-` row names research review,
design review or plan review. `feature-pipeline/SKILL.md:385-386` states the same tally in its own
words: "diff review found none".

**Against removing:** in the one cycle actually measured, the gates found things the ledger's `FX-`
table never sees because they were caught before a defect existed. `FEAT-G1`'s security cases
constrained the 404 body; `FEAT-G2` found a guarantee that was **vacuous** — the exact class
`ADR-0010` exists to close; `FEAT-G4` found "a cases doc describing the inverse of its own check"
(§3) and "a **fifth copy site** in the service that the plan had not named" (§7.1). A second ledger
table, "Found during implementation, before the code reached a branch" (§7.2), credits **plan review
×1** with a real find: three functional cases whose DoD was unreachable.

### What each option costs mechanically

These are not optional extras; they fail `pnpm verify` if skipped.

- **The gate ID is retired in place, never deleted.** `FEAT-G1`…`FEAT-G4` are cited by the existing
  profiling record, and `AR-API-10` fails a record naming a stage `docs/process.md` does not define
  (`FACT-3451`, `ADR-0020`). The row stays, marked retired, the way `FEAT-S5` is.
- **The reviewer role.** Either keep both the `.claude/agents/*.md` file and the `team-roles` row (a
  role that is never dispatched), or remove both — `AR-API-07`/`AR-API-08` compare them in both
  directions.
- **2a, 2b and 2c need a new ADR superseding `ADR-0016`.** `FACT-3306`, `FACT-3307` and `FACT-3308`
  each name the gate for research, design and plan respectively
  (`docs/adr/ADR-0016-discovery-stages.md:35-41`). Under `ADR-0022` the affected fact is **retired**
  into a `## Retired facts` register naming its successor, `pnpm fact:lock` is regenerated, and
  `AR-API-15`/`AR-API-16`/`AR-API-17` check the result. That is a whole ADR plus a corpus lifecycle
  operation per gate removed, and it is part of what the owner approves.
- **2d needs no superseding ADR.** No keyed fact names the code-review gate; `FACT-3267` and
  `FACT-3271` name the `code-reviewer` **role**, not the gate. The edits are `docs/process.md:34`,
  `feature-pipeline` §7, and `requesting-code-review/SKILL.md`.

**The asymmetry is worth stating plainly: `FEAT-G4` is the cheapest gate to remove procedurally and
the most expensive in what it found; `FEAT-G3` is the opposite.**

### Expected effect — projected

Per option, the certain saving is the gate's own row: **2a 158,585 / 8.2 min; 2b 151,795 / 8.0 min;
2c 126,135 / 6.1 min; 2d 159,360 / 5.8 min. All four: 595,875 tokens, 17.1%, and 28.1 of 190
agent-minutes.** Any further saving depends on defects not being found, which is not a saving.

### My recommendation

**Hold all four on this evidence, and if one must go, take 2c.** The reason is not conservatism: it is
that `FEAT-G3` is the only gate whose measured blockers changed no shape **and** whose class of find
(task ordering, DoD reachability) has a cheaper successor already in the flow — `FEAT-S9` test design
and `FEAT-S10`'s red run now sit between the plan and the implementation and both fail loudly on an
unreachable DoD. That successor is itself unmeasured, which is the honest caveat.

If the owner wants `FEAT-G3` cheaper rather than gone, **P5 offers the same gate at a lower model
price** and is the smaller step.

### Do nothing

The gates keep costing 595,875 tokens and 28.1 agent-minutes per cycle, 17.1% of the run, and keep
returning `accept after blockers`. That is a known price for a known yield, and one record does not
show it is a bad trade.

## P3 — `FEAT-S6` acceptance is run by `lead` directly, with no dispatch

### What changes

1. `docs/process.md:35` — `FEAT-S6`'s Role cell becomes `lead`; the Output cell is unchanged ("one
   green `pnpm verify`, reported with numbers").
2. `.claude/skills/team-roles/SKILL.md:38` — `tester-acceptance`'s Owns cell drops "the `pnpm verify`
   gate" and keeps `FEAT-S7` and `FEAT-S8`, which need `Write`.
3. `.claude/skills/feature-pipeline/SKILL.md` §8 — acceptance is run by the orchestrator through the
   `regression-verify` skill rather than by a dispatched agent.
4. `.claude/agents/lead.md` — the stage is named in its sequence. **No tool changes:** `lead` already
   holds `Bash` (§5), and running a command writes no file, so `FACT-3264` ("cannot, by tools: write
   files") is untouched.

`tester-acceptance` stays a role, keeps its file and its `team-roles` row, and is still dispatched for
`FEAT-S7`/`FEAT-S8` — so `AR-API-07`/`AR-API-08` are untouched.

### The evidence

- `FEAT-S6` cost **286,179 tokens, 67 tool calls and 15.0 agent-minutes across 2 dispatches** (§2.1)
  to produce "one green `pnpm verify`" — a command `e2e/README.md:269-270` prices at **47–66 seconds**,
  or ~137 s on a cold `.next` (§8).
- That is **8.2% of the run's tokens and 7.9% of its agent-minutes** for one command. Per dispatch,
  arithmetic over the cited cells: ~143,090 tokens.
- The second dispatch is rework: `FEAT-S6` appears in the rework row caught at `FEAT-G4` (§3).
- `lead` holds `Read, Grep, Glob, Bash, Agent, Skill` (§5) — every tool the stage needs.

### Expected effect — projected

**Up to 286,179 tokens (8.2%) and 15.0 agent-minutes (7.9%) leave the dispatch table**, and the cycle
loses two of its 29 dispatches.

**The honest caveat, and it is large:** `lead` has no row in the profiling record (§2.6, "orchestrator;
no row in the record"). Running acceptance in the orchestrator's own context **moves** the cost to a
place the record does not count as much as it removes it. Without **P6a** this proposal will look like
a saving whether or not it is one. I recommend approving P3 only together with P6a; it is the one place
in this plan where I am asking for a coupling, and this is the justification.

### What it costs, and what stops being caught

- **Nothing stops being caught.** `pnpm verify` runs, with the same eight steps, at the same point in
  the order. The check is identical; the reader changes.
- What is lost is a **separate pair of eyes on the run output**. A dedicated agent reports failures
  without having sequenced the work; the orchestrator reports on a run of work it dispatched. The
  measured cycle already recorded two process errors the orchestrator made that no gate caught —
  acceptance run before code review, and four tasks nearly dispatched in parallel on a false plan line
  (§2.5). Putting acceptance inside the orchestrator puts one more thing in the place where those
  errors happened.
- **It brushes `ADR-0014`'s rationale without contradicting its facts.** `FACT-3275` rejects "letting
  the lead implement when it is faster — then nothing gates the work, and the lead's judgement is about
  its own code." `lead` writes no code here, so the stated reason does not apply; but the owner should
  know the boundary is being approached. No fact is retired and no superseding ADR is required. If the
  owner wants it airtight, a new ADR recording the exception is the alternative, and it costs a record.

### How the next record shows whether it worked

Two numbers, both in the next record: **`FEAT-S6`'s row disappears from the dispatch table**, and the
new orchestrator row (P6a) shows what the run cost inside `lead`. **The test: orchestrator row for
acceptance < 143,090 tokens**, the measured per-dispatch figure. If the orchestrator row is at or above
that, the cost moved and nothing was saved — revert.

### Do nothing

Every cycle keeps spending ~286,000 tokens and 15 agent-minutes dispatching an agent to run a
66-second command. That is the second-cheapest structural saving in this plan and the least risky, so
"do nothing" here is a real loss rather than a neutral option.

## P4 — Drop `FEAT-S6` entirely for changes below a named size

**This is the lever §12 records as refused in round one** with the reason: "Not proposed because
`ADR-0014` rejected self-grading by name and would need a superseding ADR"
(`…tuning.plan.md:362-366`).

### What changes

`docs/process.md:35` gains a condition — `FEAT-S6` runs only for a change above a named size (the
`bugfix-pipeline` §4 threshold is the only size rule the repository already has). Below it, the
implementer's own green `pnpm verify` is the acceptance. `feature-pipeline` §8 changes accordingly; it
currently says the opposite in as many words ("the earlier wording here — 'for a small feature the
implementer's DoD _is_ the acceptance' — is exactly the self-grading `ADR-0014` removed",
`SKILL.md:318-322`).

### The evidence

- `FEAT-S6` = 286,179 tokens, 15.0 agent-minutes, 8.2% (§2.1).
- **No record of a small `FEAT` cycle exists** (§11), so there is no measurement of what this saves on
  the changes it would apply to. The 286,179 figure is from a full-size feature and is an overstatement
  of the saving on a small one.

### What it costs, and what stops being caught

**An acceptance run has found two defects in the ledger's 36 `FX-` rows — `FX-005` and `FX-030` — plus
`FX-004` jointly with code review (§7.2). That is more than the review gates' one.** Dropping the
stage removes the check that has the second-best yield in the whole ledger, on exactly the changes
nobody is watching closely.

Mechanically it needs **a new ADR superseding `ADR-0014`**: `FACT-3268` assigns running tests and
reporting results to the `tester-*` roles, and `feature-pipeline` §8 records the removal of this exact
wording as a decision. Under `ADR-0022` the affected fact is retired with a successor and the lock
regenerated.

### Expected effect — projected

**Up to 286,179 tokens and 15.0 agent-minutes on a change large enough to have been measured; unknown
and smaller on the changes it actually applies to.**

### My recommendation

**No.** P3 removes the dispatch and keeps the run, capturing most of the same tokens without removing
a check that has found two ledger defects, and without a superseding ADR. If the owner wants the
saving, P3 is the same saving at a fraction of the risk.

### How the next record would show whether it worked

There is no number that shows this worked — only one that shows it failed: an `FX-` row whose "Found
by" cell names a run after the change shipped, on a change that took the short path. That is the
falsification, and it arrives late by construction.

### Do nothing

P3, if approved, already takes most of this saving. If neither is approved, acceptance keeps costing
8.2% per cycle.

## P5 — Change a role's model

**This is the lever §12 records as refused in round one**, with the reason quoted there: "the records
measure **tokens, not price**" (`…tuning.plan.md:368-373`).

### What changes

The `model` field on line 5 of one or more files in `.claude/agents/`, and the matching Model cell in
`team-roles/SKILL.md:17-41`. **Both must change together**: `AR-API-08` compares the two by role
**name** only — the Tools and Model columns are prose (§5) — so nothing mechanical catches a drift
here and review has to.

### The evidence

- **12 roles run on `opus`, 10 on `sonnet`, 1 on `haiku`** (§5).
- The rows whose Model cell reads `opus` total **1,328,298 tokens, 38.2%** of the run; `sonnet` rows

- **The record measures tokens, not price. `docs/profiling/README.md`'s protocol has no price column**

- `team-roles/SKILL.md:52-53` states the assignment rule: "`opus` is for the roles where one mistake
  costs an iteration — deciding, judging, and security. `sonnet` is for work bounded by an accepted
  artifact or by a convention."

### The candidates, with a recommendation each

Only six `opus` rows have a measured baseline at all; a model change on a role with no baseline cannot

| `research-reviewer` | 158,585 tok / 8.2 min  | judging, and it found the two security cases                                                                   | no — security                               |
| `design-reviewer`   | 151,795 tok / 8.0 min  | judging; it found a vacuous guarantee                                                                          | no                                          |
| `designer`          | 428,268 tok / 21.7 min | deciding the shape                                                                                             | no — a mistake here costs an iteration      |
| `planner`           | 304,155 tok / 29.3 min | deciding                                                                                                       | no                                          |
| `test-designer`     | **none**               | bounded by an accepted plan and design — fits `sonnet` exactly                                                 | defensible, but unfalsifiable: no baseline  |
| `tester-security`   | inside a `mixed` row   | security                                                                                                       | no — `team-roles:52` names security by name |
| `pipeline-reviewer` | none                   | retrieval-heavy, but this round's output is 741 dense cited lines                                              | no                                          |

### Expected effect — projected

**Zero tokens.** The saving is monetary and this repository holds no number for it. With **P6b**
approved, the next record carries a cost column and the effect becomes measurable for the first time;
without P6b, this proposal is unfalsifiable by construction.

Secondary, and it can go either way: a cheaper model on `plan-reviewer` may raise the token count by
iterating more, or lower it. The measured baseline of 126,135 tokens and 2 blockers is what the next
record is compared against.

### What it costs, and what stops being caught

A weaker model on a gate is a weaker gate. `FEAT-G3`'s two measured blockers were commit-ordering
errors — the kind bounded by an accepted artifact — but the ledger's second table credits plan review
with catching an unreachable DoD across two features (§7.2), which is a judgement about the whole plan,
not a lookup.

No ADR is superseded: `FACT-3272` states that models are assigned per role, which is what continues to
happen.

### How the next record shows whether it worked

With P6b: the `FEAT-G3` row's **cost** cell against the run total, and its **blockers** cell. **The
test: `FEAT-G3` still raises blockers of the same class at a lower cost.** If the next record shows
`FEAT-G3` passing clean while a later stage finds a plan-level error, the model change caused it and
should be reverted.

### Do nothing

The model assignment stays as `ADR-0014` set it. Nothing breaks; `opus` rows stay at 38.2% of tokens
and an unknown share of price. Given that no price number exists anywhere in the repository, "do
nothing" here is defensible until P6b has produced one record.

## P6 — Two cells in the profiling protocol: an orchestrator row and a cost column

**This adds to a record, not to the flow.** It is two cells per cycle, written by the role that already
writes the record.

### What changes

`docs/profiling/README.md`, the protocol section (currently lines 23-37):

- **6a** — the per-stage table gains a row for the **orchestrator** (`lead`), covering the work done in
  the orchestrator's own context rather than in a dispatch.
- **6b** — the table gains a **cost** column beside the tokens column, so a model change has a unit.

Existing records are **append-only** and are not rewritten (`FACT-3443`, `pipeline-tuning/SKILL.md:99-100`).

### The evidence

- `lead` is "orchestrator; no row in the record" (§2.6). Of 21 inventory rows, 11 have no figure, and
  the orchestrator's own work has no figure at all.
- "The record measures **tokens, not price** — no price column exists in `docs/profiling/README.md`'s
  protocol" (§5).
- `FEAT-S8` "is written by the orchestrator and is not separately measured" (§2.1, record line 32),
  and `ADR-0020:92` says of that cost "The cost is real and is paid on every run".

### Why this addition is worth its cost on every future change

Two cells per cycle, in a document that is written once per cycle by a role already writing it.
Against that: without 6a, **P3 cannot be distinguished from cost-hiding**, and without 6b, **no model
change in this repository can ever be shown to have worked or failed**. Both are the difference
between a change that can be reverted on evidence and one that cannot. That argument holds for every
future cycle, not only this one.

### What it costs, and what stops being caught

Nothing stops being caught; no check changes. The cost is that the orchestrator must attribute its own
consumption, which it has not had to do before — and the record's own `FX-034` shows the orchestrator
already miscounted its own table once (dispatches 30 → 29; agent-time 190 against a column summing to
190.5, §1). A row that is guessed is worse than no row. The protocol should say the cell is left blank
rather than estimated when the number is not available.

### How the next record shows whether it worked
