# Tuning plan 2026-09-29 — `TUNE-S2`

**The evidence behind this plan is one profiling record.** `docs/profiling/runs/` holds exactly one
file, `2026-09-28-meetings-detail-participants.md`, and the review that reads it
(`docs/profiling/reviews/2026-09-29-review.md`) says on its first line that nothing in it is a
trend. One record supports "watch this next time"; it does not support rebuilding how every future
change is made. Every proposal below is written to be reversible on the next record's numbers, and
half of them change nothing about how work is done — they make the two largest cost centres
attributable, so that the _next_ round of tuning can aim.

What this rests on:

- The review `docs/profiling/reviews/2026-09-29-review.md` (`TUNE-S1`, accepted).
- Through it, the single record for `meetings-detail-participants` (`FT-003`), commit `48d9256`:
  3,478,421 tokens, 29 dispatches, 190 agent-minutes.
- `docs/BACKLOG.md`'s Rejected section (7 rows) and the alternatives rejected inside `ADR-0014`,
  `ADR-0016` and `ADR-0020`, all read before writing. Nothing below revives one; the last section
  names them.

The problem being planned against, in the owner's words: **a simple feature takes several hours, and
the process needs optimizing.** That is the target. It is worth saying plainly that the headline
numbers make _no_ single cut obviously right: product code was 83,098 tokens (2.4%), the tests stage
was 1,146,931 (33.0%), rework after a gate was 1,018,067 (29.3%), and the four gates together were
595,875 (17.1%). The two biggest lines are the two the record cannot break down.

Five contradictions from the review are already closed as `FX-034` (commit `91d3191`) and are not
re-proposed here.

**Every figure marked "projected" is an estimate, not a measurement.** Figures without that label
are quoted from the review.

## 1. Record the cycle's elapsed wall-clock, not only agent-time

**What changes.** `docs/profiling/README.md`, the "What every row carries" and "Four numbers"
sections: a cycle gains one further number — **elapsed wall-clock for the whole cycle**, from the
first dispatch to the ledger entry, recorded next to the agent-time total at `FEAT-S8`. The
per-stage rows are unchanged. No new stage, no new gate, no new role.

**Evidence.** Review §4, first bullet: `feature-pipeline` §10's budget is in elapsed minutes ("about
60 minutes, and that is the floor", recomputed to 70–85), the record's 190 min is agent-time summed
per dispatch — "two pairs ran in parallel, so the clock on the wall was lower" — and the review's
**Not found** line reads: no elapsed wall-clock figure for the cycle exists anywhere in
`docs/profiling/**`, `docs/process.md` or the pipeline skills.

**Expected effect.** No tokens saved. The effect is that the owner's complaint becomes a number in
the record: today "several hours" cannot be compared with anything the pipeline measures, and
`feature-pipeline` §10's 60–85 minute budget cannot be checked against a single run. Projected cost
of collecting it: one timestamp noted at the first dispatch and one subtraction at `FEAT-S8`.

**What it costs, and what stops being caught.** It adds a required number to `FEAT-S8` — the "add a
step" bias applies, in its cheapest form. `FEAT-S8`'s own cost is unmeasured today (review §4), so
the cost of this addition is projected rather than known; proposal 2 makes it measurable. Nothing
stops being caught: no check is weakened.

**How the next run falsifies it.** The next record either carries an elapsed figure beside the
190-minute-style agent-time total, or it does not. If it does, the second record gives the first
wall-clock comparison in the project's history, and `feature-pipeline` §10's budget becomes
checkable.

**Mechanical consequences.** `AR-API-10` only constrains stage IDs cited in `docs/profiling/**`;
this adds no ID. Existing records are append-only and are **not** retro-fitted (`ADR-0020`).

## 2. Split the per-stage table by first pass versus re-run, and by role where a row aggregates several

**What changes.** `docs/profiling/README.md`, the protocol: a stage that was re-run after its gate
gets **two rows with the same stage ID** — first pass and re-run — instead of one combined row; a
stage dispatched to more than one role (`FEAT-S1`'s four sweeps, `FEAT-S5`'s `tester-api`,
`tester-unit`, `tester-security`) gets one row per role. `FEAT-S8` gets a row of its own. **No new
stage IDs are minted and none is renumbered** (`ADR-0020`): the split is by role and by pass within
the same ID.

**Evidence.** Review §3: "the per-stage table does not split a stage's row between first pass and
re-run … so no first-pass-only cost is available for any stage", under a rework total of
**1,018,067 tokens (29.3%)**. Review §4: "**Not found:** any per-role split of the 1,146,931 tokens"
of `FEAT-S5` — 33.0% of the run and 66.1 of 190 agent-minutes — and "**Not found:** a token figure
for `researcher-code`, `researcher-contract`, `researcher-tests` or `researcher-history`
individually", although `research-protocol` already requires the model per sweep to be recorded.
Review §4 again: "Nothing measures the cost of `FEAT-S8` itself."

**Expected effect.** No tokens saved by this proposal itself. It makes the run's two largest cost
centres attributable to a role and a pass: the 1,018,067 rework tokens (29.3%) and the 1,146,931 of
`FEAT-S5` (33.0%) — the two figures overlap in the 407,507 recorded against `FEAT-S5`/`FEAT-S6`/the
corpus after `FEAT-G4`, and that overlap is itself a reason the numbers cannot be reasoned about as
they stand. Every cheaper-looking option the owner might reach for next — a different model on the
security tester, a shorter re-run brief, dropping a sweep — is currently un-aimable because the rows
are aggregates.

**What it costs, and what stops being caught.** More rows to fill at `FEAT-S8`, on every run, from
hand-backs that already carry per-dispatch numbers; projected addition of 4–8 rows per cycle. This
is an "add a step" proposal in the record-keeping, and it is justified only by the fact that the two
largest cost centres in the only run we have are both opaque. Nothing stops being caught.

**How the next run falsifies it.** The next record has at least two rows for each stage that was
re-run and at least three rows under `FEAT-S5`; the next `TUNE-S1` can quote a first-pass cost. If
the next record shows re-runs are cheap relative to first passes, the 29.3% rework figure stops
being an argument for anything, which is itself a result.

**Mechanical consequences.** `AR-API-10` compares cited IDs against `docs/process.md`; repeated IDs
are still valid IDs. Existing records are not rewritten.

## 3. One definition of "discovery" and "implementation", in one place

**What changes.** `docs/profiling/README.md` states the split once, by stage ID —
`FEAT-S1`…`FEAT-G3` are discovery, `FEAT-S4`…`FEAT-S8` are everything after — and
`feature-pipeline` §10 cites that definition instead of implying another. No stage table is added
anywhere (`ADR-0020`).

**Evidence.** Review §5, contradiction 11: the record's split puts `FEAT-S3` and `FEAT-G3` in the
discovery half (1,472,438 tokens, 42.3%), while `feature-pipeline` §10 puts "plan 10, plan review
12" inside the implementation half's 60-minute floor. "The two definitions of 'discovery' and
'implementation' do not cover the same stages."

**Expected effect.** No tokens saved. The two headline numbers of the first record (42.3% / 57.7%)
become comparable with the second record and with §10's budget. Without this, the first cross-run
comparison this project ever makes will compare two different partitions of the same stages.

**What it costs, and what stops being caught.** One sentence in each of two files. Nothing stops
being caught. The risk is the opposite one: if the split is fixed in the wrong place, later records
inherit it — hence naming it by stage ID rather than by prose, so a later change to it is visible.

**How the next run falsifies it.** The next `TUNE-S1` review finds one definition rather than two,
and can put the two records' discovery shares side by side.

## 4. Budget `design.md` by length, the way the plan is already budgeted — and do **not** budget the research files

**What changes.** `design-protocol` gains a length budget for `design.md` — proposed **300 lines**
(projected as a target, not derived from a measurement) — in the same form `feature-pipeline` §4
already uses for the plan ("**100–150 lines**"), with the exactness requirements named as what is
never cut: the contract's exact error bodies, the seed as concrete values, and the rejected
alternatives. `research-protocol` is **not** changed; see below.

**Evidence.** Review §2: `design.md` on disk is **697 lines**, against a plan budgeted at 100–150 and
measured at 149. Review §4: "**No document budgets the size of `design.md` or of a research file**"
— `design-protocol` and `research-protocol` were read in full and state no length rule. `FEAT-S2` is
the largest discovery line at **428,268 tokens and 21.7 min**, plus **257,359 tokens of rework**
after `FEAT-G2` — together 19.7% of the run. `feature-pipeline` §4 already carries the argument for
length limits on the other artifact: "The longer the plan, the more of it is work unrelated to the
feature", with three of nine and both of two blockers in the first iteration being bookkeeping
introduced by edits to the document itself.

**Why research is excluded.** `FEAT-G1`'s shape-changing blocker was that the sweep **never opened**
two security cases, which then constrained the 404 body (review §2, gate yield). That is a research
file that was too thin, not too fat. Budgeting research length down would push against the one gate
finding that changed the shape of the work at the cheapest stage in the run.

**Expected effect.** `design.md` for the next feature of comparable size comes in at **≤300 lines**
(projected), against 697. The token effect on `FEAT-S2` is **unknown and is not claimed**: the review
records it as an open question — "Whether `FEAT-S2`'s 428,268 tokens relate to `design.md`'s 697
lines in any measured way. **Not found:** any per-line or per-artifact cost figure."

**What it costs, and what stops being caught.** A budget invites cutting the wrong thing. The
specific risk is `FEAT-G2`'s other blocker class — "a mechanical guarantee claimed for a check that
does not perform it" — which is caught by precision, not by length, and precision is what the named
exceptions protect. If the next design hits the budget by dropping exact error bodies, invariants 1
and 8 are the ones exposed, and `FEAT-G2` is where that shows up as a blocker rather than as shipped
code.

**How the next run falsifies it.** Two numbers in the next record: the line count of `design.md` in
the change folder, and `FEAT-S2`'s tokens and rework. If the design comes in at 300 lines and
`FEAT-S2` costs the same or more, the length hypothesis is wrong and the budget should go.

## 5. While localizing a defect inside `FEAT-S5`, runs are filtered; the full suite runs once to confirm

**What changes.** `playwright-verify` (the single-change procedure) states that while reproducing and
fixing a defect found during `FEAT-S5`, runs are filtered — `pnpm test:<feature>`, a `--grep` or one
Playwright project — and the full `pnpm e2e` is run **once** to confirm the fix. Acceptance's
unfiltered run stays where it is, at `FEAT-S6`. This restates no existing rule: `CLAUDE.md`'s
"who runs what" governs `pnpm test` by hand and acceptance, not the debug loop inside a stage.

**Evidence.** Review §2, "Where the record says the time went": `FEAT-S5` ran 66.1 minutes, "of which
**24 minutes was `FX-033`… reproduced, fixed and verified across twelve full suite runs**".
`e2e/README.md` measures one `pnpm e2e` at 27–45 s and one `pnpm verify` at 47–66 s (measured
2026-09-29). `FEAT-S5` is the single largest line in the run at 1,146,931 tokens (33.0%).

**Expected effect.** Projected: eleven of those twelve full runs become filtered runs. At the
measured 27–45 s per full `pnpm e2e`, that is **5.0–8.3 minutes of run time removed** from the debug
loop, minus the cost of the filtered runs, which is unmeasured. The token effect is **not claimed**:
the review records "What share of `FEAT-S5`'s 1,146,931 tokens belongs to writing cases versus
running the suite" as an open question.

**What it costs, and what stops being caught.** A filtered run cannot see a cross-feature break
introduced by the fix. That is exactly what `FEAT-S6`'s one green `pnpm verify` is for, and it is
unchanged — so the check is moved later, not removed. The residual risk is a longer feedback loop on
a cross-feature break: found at `FEAT-S6` instead of at the eighth of twelve runs. The record gives
no instance of that having happened.

**How the next run falsifies it.** The next record's `FEAT-S5` row and note: the number of full suite
runs during the stage (target ≤2, against twelve) and the stage's agent-minutes. If `FEAT-S6` comes
back red with a cross-feature break for the first time, this proposal is the suspect and reverting it
is one sentence.

## 8. Settle whether acceptance needs its own agent — in `regression-verify`

**What changes.** `regression-verify` loses the paragraph beginning "For a feature the size of 'a page
plus two endpoints' a **separate acceptance agent is unnecessary**: the implementer's DoD with
control experiments _is_ the acceptance", which contradicts `feature-pipeline` §8 and
`docs/process.md`. The rule that survives is the one `feature-pipeline` §8 already states: acceptance
is `tester-acceptance`'s stage, and it is **one** `pnpm verify`, not a second walk through every
level.

**Evidence.** Review §5, contradiction 8, quoting both sides in full, and noting that in the measured
run `FEAT-S6` was a separate `tester-acceptance` dispatch costing **286,179 tokens and 15.0 min**
(8.2% of the run). `feature-pipeline` §8 names the removed wording as "exactly the self-grading
`ADR-0014` removed".

**Expected effect.** No tokens saved; `FEAT-S6` keeps costing about 286,179 tokens per cycle. What
changes is that the orchestrator can no longer read one skill as permission to skip a stage that
`docs/process.md` defines. This is the direction that keeps the cheaper-looking option **available**
to the owner rather than taken silently by an agent: see the last section for what dropping
`FEAT-S6` would cost and require.

**What it costs, and what stops being caught.** It closes a wording that could have saved 15 minutes
per small feature. That saving is real and is not dismissed — it is refused **here** because
`ADR-0014` rejected self-grading by name, and an accepted ADR is changed by a superseding record, not
by a skill paragraph.

**How the next run falsifies it.** The next record's `FEAT-S6` row: if it again shows a green
`pnpm verify` and zero blockers, two records of a gate that has never caught anything become an
argument the owner can act on with a superseding ADR. The purpose of this proposal is to make that
argument out of evidence instead of out of a contradiction.

## 9. Remove the "the plan review is skipped along with the plan" clause from `requesting-code-review`

**What changes.** `requesting-code-review` §"When not to": the clause "A change the size of 'a page
plus two endpoints', documentation, config, renames — a code review is enough; the plan review is
skipped along with the plan" is replaced by the rule that actually holds — stages shrink with the
task but are not skipped (`docs/process.md`, `feature-pipeline` §"Phases") — with the existing
below-threshold short path for defects (`bugfix-pipeline` §4) named as the one place where stages are
skipped by design. The adapter stays self-contained without `.agents/`.

**Evidence.** Review §5, contradiction 9, both sides quoted. `requesting-code-review` is also the
document whose framing the whole four-gate policy rests on (§"Why the policy differs").

**Expected effect.** No tokens saved. It removes the only text in the repository that permits an
orchestrator to skip a defined stage for a feature.

**What it costs, and what stops being caught.** This is the one proposal here that can make work
**slower**: for a genuinely small feature it removes a written permission to skip `FEAT-S3` and
`FEAT-G3` — 304,155 + 126,135 tokens in the measured cycle, on a change that was not small. **No
record exists of a small `FEAT` cycle**, so neither the saving nor the risk of skipping has a
measurement behind it. The alternative — defining a below-threshold `FEAT` path the way
`bugfix-pipeline` §4 defines one for defects — is a real option and is deliberately **not** proposed
on one record of one large change; it is on the watch list below.

**How the next run falsifies it.** If the next cycle is a small feature and its `FEAT-S3` + `FEAT-G3`
cost is a large share of a small total, that is the evidence for a below-threshold `FEAT` path, and
this clause's removal is what forces the question to be asked as a proposal rather than settled by an
agent mid-run.

## What I deliberately did not propose, and why

**Removing any of the four gates.** They total 595,875 tokens (17.1%) and 28.1 of 190 agent-minutes,
which is the largest single saving available without touching the tests stage. Against that, the
gate-yield table: `FEAT-G1` found two security cases the sweep never opened, and they constrained
the 404 body; `FEAT-G2` found the seed-mirror guarantee was vacuous, and the fix made it real;
`FEAT-G3` caught a definition of done unreachable at its own commit; `FEAT-G4` found a fifth copy
site in the service that the plan had not named. Two of eight blockers changed the shape of the
work. The review also records that the run "cannot distinguish" whether four `accept after blockers`
verdicts mean the gates are catching real defects or the stages are run loosely because a gate is
known to follow — and one record cannot distinguish them either.

**Dropping `FEAT-S6` (acceptance) for small changes.** Projected saving: 286,179 tokens and 15.0 min
per cycle, 8.2%. Not proposed because `ADR-0014` rejected self-grading by name and would need a
superseding ADR, and because one record in which `FEAT-S6` raised no blockers is not evidence that it
never will. Proposal 8 is the version of this that keeps the option open and makes the evidence
accumulate.

**Changing any role's model.** The `opus` rows total 1,328,298 tokens (38.2%), which looks like the
obvious lever. The review records "What the same cycle would have cost with a different model
assignment" as an open question — no run exists with a different assignment — and the records measure
**tokens, not price**, so a swap to a cheaper model could raise the number the record tracks while
lowering what it costs. Evaluating a model change needs either a second record or a price column, and
inventing a price column on one record is not supported.

**A machine check that a profiling record exists.** The review's §4 notes that nothing checks it,
while the ledger entry is checked by `LG-API-*`. This is the cheapest "add a step" in the whole plan
and is refused for that reason: the record was written, on the one cycle that had to write one. If a
cycle ever ends without a record, this is the first candidate.

**Making `research/probes.md` a scaffolded file.** `feature-pipeline` §0 mandates probes,
`research-protocol`'s folder listing does not mention them, and the change folder has a 197-line
`research/probes.md` anyway (review §4). Scaffolding it would make `PR-API-05` require it on every
change — a required artifact paid for by every future change to fix a documentation gap with no
measured cost.

**Budgeting the research files.** 197–400 lines each, no rule — see proposal 4 for why the gate
yield points the other way.

**A below-threshold `FEAT` path** (the `bugfix-pipeline` §4 analogue for small features). Named in
proposal 9 as the alternative it forecloses. Not proposed because no record of a small `FEAT` cycle
exists; it is the first thing the second record could support.

**Anything from `docs/BACKLOG.md`'s Rejected section.** All seven rows were read: mutation testing,
`fullyParallel: false`, deleting `docs/plans/plan-review-*.md`, a state reset endpoint,
`pnpm audit --audit-level=low`, unit coverage for `server-only` modules, and jsdom component tests.
Nothing in the single record overturns any of the stated reasons, and none is revived here.

**Anything rejected inside the ADRs that built this pipeline.** `ADR-0016` rejected folding research
into the `planner`, one reviewer for all three artifacts, and machine-checking research for
speculation; `ADR-0014` rejected one general-purpose agent, a reviewer with `Edit`, and letting the
lead implement "when it is faster"; `ADR-0020` rejected putting the inventory in `CLAUDE.md`, putting
profiling in `e2e/README.md`, and deriving the numbers automatically from transcripts. None is
revived.

**Two one-line corrections below this flow's threshold.** `e2e/README.md`'s "110 e2e in 13 files"
against "`pnpm e2e` — all 109" eight lines below (review §5, contradiction 14), and `ADR-0014:61`'s
description of what `AR-API-07` checks against the cases doc's assignment of it to `AR-API-08`
(contradiction 12). `pipeline-tuning` says it outright: "The change is one word in one skill. Fix it
and note it; three agents and a human gate for a typo is the shape `CH-004` warned about, applied to
itself." They belong in the next docs commit, not on this list.
